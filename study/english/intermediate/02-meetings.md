# Study: Meetings — Joining and Speaking Up

Meetings are where teams decide things. This lesson helps you speak up politely: agree, disagree, and suggest ideas.

## 1. Easy explanation
In a meeting, you listen and you speak. You need polite ways to **agree**, **disagree**, **suggest**, and **ask for clarity** — without sounding rude.

## 3. Useful phrases (ready to use)
- May I add something?
- **In my opinion**, we should…
- I **agree** with **[name]**.
- I see your **point**, but…
- I'd like to **suggest** a different idea.
- Could you **clarify** that, please?
- Let's move to the next **point**.

## 4. A real-life conversation
**In a team meeting.**
- **Chair:** Let's discuss the deadline. Any thoughts?
- **You:** May I add something? In my opinion, Friday is too soon.
- **Colleague:** I see your point, but the client needs it early.
- **You:** I agree the client is important. I'd like to suggest Monday instead. Could you clarify what the client really needs first?
- **Chair:** Good point. Let's confirm with the client, then decide.

## 5. Small grammar tip — polite disagreement
Never say a flat "No, you're wrong." Soften it:
- "I **see your point, but**…"
- "That's true, **however**…"
- "I'm not sure I agree. **Perhaps** we could…"

## 6. Practice — say it and write it
1. Say 3 times: "I see your point, but I'd like to suggest…"
2. Write 3 polite meeting lines: one to agree, one to disagree, one to suggest.
3. Write one line to ask for clarity.

## 7. Common mistakes (and the fix)
- ❌ "I am agree." → ✅ "I agree."
- ❌ Interrupting: cutting in loudly. → ✅ "May I add something?"
- ❌ "You are wrong." → ✅ "I see your point, but…"

## 8. Tips to remember
- Ask permission to speak: **"May I add something?"**
- Agree first, then differ: **"I agree that…, but…"**
- Keep points short — meetings value clear, quick ideas.

## 9. Practice questions / tasks
1. Fix: "I am agree with you."
2. Give a polite way to disagree.
3. How do you ask someone to explain more?
