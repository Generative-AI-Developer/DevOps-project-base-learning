# Study: Making and Handling Requests Politely

At work you ask people for things, and they ask you. This lesson helps you ask politely, offer help, and say "no" kindly.

## 1. Easy explanation
A polite **request** makes people want to help. And when you must say **no**, you do it kindly and offer an **alternative** (another option). This keeps good relationships.

## 3. Useful phrases (ready to use)
- **Would you mind** helping me with this?
- **Could you possibly** send it today?
- **May I ask a favor?**
- I'd be **happy to** help.
- **Unfortunately**, I can't right now.
- I'm **afraid** I'm **unable** to do that, **but** I can **[alternative]**.
- Can we find an **alternative**?

## 4. A real-life conversation
**Asking a favor, and politely refusing another.**
- **You:** May I ask a favor? Would you mind checking my report?
- **Colleague:** Not at all. I'd be happy to.
- **You:** Thank you, I appreciate it.
- **Colleague:** By the way, can you finish it by 2 p.m.?
- **You:** Unfortunately, I can't by 2. But I can have it ready by 4. Would that work?
- **Colleague:** 4 is fine. Thanks.

## 5. Small grammar tip — "Would you mind + -ing?"
After **"Would you mind"**, use the **-ing** form:
- ✅ "Would you mind **helping** me?"  (not "to help")
- To agree, say: **"Not at all"** or **"Sure, no problem."** (this means "Yes, I'll do it").

## 6. Practice — say it and write it
1. Say 3 times: "Would you mind helping me with this? — Not at all."
2. Write 2 polite requests and 1 polite refusal (with an alternative).
3. Write one line offering to help.

## 7. Common mistakes (and the fix)
- ❌ "Would you mind to help?" → ✅ "Would you mind **helping**?"
- ❌ Refusing with just "No." → ✅ "Unfortunately I can't now, **but** I can… later."
- ❌ "Give me a favor." → ✅ "May I **ask** a favor?"

## 8. Tips to remember
- Soft openers: **"Would you mind…?"**, **"Could you possibly…?"**
- Say no + **but** + alternative.
- "Not at all" = a **yes** to a "Would you mind…?" question.

## 9. Practice questions / tasks
1. Fix: "Would you mind to send it?"
2. Refuse politely and offer another time.
3. Offer to help a colleague.
