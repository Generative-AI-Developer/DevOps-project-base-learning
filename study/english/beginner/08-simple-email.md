# Study: Write a Simple Email

Email is the most common way to write at work. This lesson shows you the simple, polite parts of an email so you can write one with confidence.

## 1. Easy explanation
An email has clear parts: a **subject** (the topic), a **greeting** ("Dear…"), the **body** (your message), and a **closing** ("Best regards"). Keep it short, clear, and polite.

## 3. Useful phrases (ready to use)
- Dear **[name]**,
- I hope you are well.
- I am writing to **[reason]**.
- Please find the **attachment** below.
- **Kindly confirm** by **[time]**.
- Thank you for your time.
- Best regards,
  **[your name]**

## 4. A sample simple email
```
Subject: Request for Leave on Friday

Dear Mr. Khan,

I hope you are well. I am writing to request one day of leave
on Friday, 20th June, for a family matter.

I will finish my urgent tasks before Friday. Kindly confirm if
this is okay.

Thank you for your time.

Best regards,
Ali Ahmed
```

## 5. Small grammar tip — capital letters
Start every sentence with a **capital letter**. Use capitals for **names** and **days/months**:
- *Dear **M**r. **K**han,*  ·  *on **F**riday*  ·  *in **J**une*

## 6. Practice — say it and write it
1. Read the sample email out loud once.
2. Change it to request a **half day** instead of a full day.
3. Write a subject line for an email that sends a report.

## 7. Common mistakes (and the fix)
- ❌ No subject line. → ✅ Always write a short, clear **subject**.
- ❌ Starting with "Hey" at work. → ✅ Use "Dear [name]," or "Hello [name],".
- ❌ No closing. → ✅ End with "Best regards," and your name.

## 8. Tips to remember
- Structure: **Subject → Dear → Body → Best regards → Name**.
- Keep it **short**: 3–5 sentences is often enough.
- Read it once before you **send** — check names and dates.

## 9. Practice questions / tasks
1. Name the four main parts of an email.
2. Write a polite closing.
3. What is the "subject" of an email?
