# Study: Asking and Giving Directions

In a new office or building, you must ask where things are, and sometimes tell others the way. This lesson gives you the words for directions.

## 1. Easy explanation
**Directions** tell you which way to go: left, right, straight. You ask with "Where is…?" and answer with simple steps like "Go straight, then turn left."

## 3. Useful phrases (ready to use)
- Excuse me, where is the **[place]**?
- How do I get to the **[place]**?
- Go straight, then turn left.
- It's on the second **floor**.
- It's **next to** the reception.
- Take the **lift** to the third floor.
- It's **opposite** the manager's office.

## 4. A real-life conversation
**Asking for the meeting room.**
- **You:** Excuse me, where is the meeting room?
- **Guard:** Go straight down this hall, then turn right.
- **You:** Is it on this floor?
- **Guard:** No, take the lift to the second floor. It's next to the reception.
- **You:** Thank you very much.
- **Guard:** You're welcome.

## 5. Small grammar tip — directions use commands
Directions are **imperatives** (commands). No "you", just the verb:
- **Go** straight. **Turn** left. **Take** the lift. **Stop** at the corner.

## 6. Practice — say it and write it
1. Say 3 times: "Go straight, then turn left. It's next to the reception."
2. Write directions from the entrance to your desk (3–4 steps).
3. Ask for two places: the exit and the manager's office.

## 7. Common mistakes (and the fix)
- ❌ "Where is meeting room?" → ✅ "Where is **the** meeting room?"
- ❌ "Turn on left." → ✅ "Turn **left**." (no "on")
- ❌ "Go to straight." → ✅ "Go **straight**." (no "to")

## 8. Tips to remember
- Point with your hand while you say **left / right** — it helps you remember.
- Directions are short commands: Go, Turn, Take, Stop.
- Add "the" before places: **the** exit, **the** room.

## 9. Practice questions / tasks
1. Fix: "Where is exit?"
2. Give a direction using "next to".
3. Say how to reach the second floor.
