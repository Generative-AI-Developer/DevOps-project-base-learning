# Study: Networking and Professional Etiquette

**Networking** means building useful professional relationships. **Etiquette** means good manners. This lesson helps you make a strong, professional impression.

## 1. Easy explanation
When you meet new professional people (at events, online, or at work), a polite, confident manner builds your **reputation**. You greet warmly, exchange **contacts**, and **follow up** later to stay in touch.

## 3. Useful phrases (ready to use)
- It's a **pleasure** to meet you.
- Here's my **business card**.
- May I **connect** with you on LinkedIn?
- Let's **stay in touch**.
- I'll **follow up** by email.
- Thank you for the **introduction**.
- I'd be happy to **refer** you if I hear of anything.

## 4. A real-life conversation
**Networking at an event.**
- **You:** Hello, I'm Ali, a software developer. It's a pleasure to meet you.
- **New contact:** Nice to meet you too, Ali. I'm Sara, from a design company.
- **You:** That's interesting. We might work well together. Here's my card.
- **Sara:** Thank you. May I connect with you on LinkedIn?
- **You:** Of course. Let's stay in touch. I'll follow up by email this week.
- **Sara:** Great. Thank you.

## 5. Small grammar tip — formal, warm openers
Professional openers are warm but formal:
- "**It's a pleasure to** meet you."
- "**I'd be happy to** help / refer you."
- "**May I** connect with you…?"

## 6. Practice — say it and write it
1. Say 3 times: "It's a pleasure to meet you. Let's stay in touch."
2. Write a short networking conversation (5–6 lines).
3. Write a one-line follow-up message after meeting someone.

## 7. Common mistakes (and the fix)
- ❌ Too casual with new contacts ("Hey, what's up?"). → ✅ "It's a pleasure to meet you."
- ❌ Not following up. → ✅ Always send a short follow-up: "Great to meet you today…"
- ❌ Only taking, never giving. → ✅ Offer help too: "I'd be happy to refer you."

## 8. Tips to remember
- First impression: warm, polite, **confident**.
- Always **follow up** within a day or two.
- Give value too (offer help), don't only ask.

## 9. Practice questions / tasks
1. Greet a new professional contact politely.
2. Ask to connect on LinkedIn.
3. Write a short follow-up message.
