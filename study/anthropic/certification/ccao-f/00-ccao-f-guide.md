# Study: CCAO-F Key Facts

> **Words to know**
> - **Generative AI** — AI that creates new text, images, or code.
> - **LLM** — Large Language Model. In simple words: a very big text-prediction helper.
> - **Token** — a small piece of text; you pay per token.
> - **Context window** — how much text the model can read at once.
> - **Hallucination** — when the model says something that sounds right but is wrong.

## 1. Easy explanation (simple → deeper)
CCAO-F checks that you understand the basics and can use Claude well and safely — no coding needed.

Know these big ideas:
- What an LLM is and what it is good/bad at.
- Tokens, context window, and models (Opus/Sonnet/Haiku).
- Good prompting (task + context + rules + examples).
- Responsible use: privacy, bias, hallucinations, and human review.

## 2. Key concepts and terms
- **What Claude is good at:** writing, summarizing, explaining, drafting, answering.
- **What to watch for:** it can be confidently wrong (hallucinate) — check important facts.
- **Prompting basics:** be specific, give context, set the format, show examples.
- **Responsible use:** do not paste private/secret data you should not share; review important outputs; know AI can be biased.

## 3. Practical examples
- Good use: "Draft a friendly reminder email; keep it short."
- Careful use: "Is this medical advice correct?" → verify with a real source; AI can be wrong.

## 4. Commands and config examples
No code for CCAO-F. It is knowledge + good judgment.

## 5. Hands-on exercises
1. Explain "token" and "context window" to a friend in one sentence each.
2. Write a strong everyday prompt for a real task.
3. List 3 things to check before trusting an AI answer.

## 6. Troubleshooting (test mindset)
- Read each question fully. Watch for "NOT" and "EXCEPT".
- Eliminate clearly wrong options first.
- Pick the safest, most responsible answer when unsure.

## 7. Common mistakes and how to avoid them
- Thinking AI is always right — it can hallucinate.
- Sharing private data without care.
- Vague prompts.

## 8. Certification notes (what the exam wants)
- Definitions (LLM, token, context, hallucination).
- Good prompting basics.
- Responsible/safe use and human oversight.

## 9. Practice questions and tasks
1. What is a hallucination?
2. Name 3 parts of a good prompt.
3. Why keep a human in the loop for important tasks?
